# Finance Agent Benchmark: method

One synthetic company, one event stream, 100 questions with exact answers. Two arms answer the same questions:

- **Arm A: raw exports and Python.** An agent gets the files a real owner would download and upload: bank CSVs,
  Stripe and Shopify reports, the bills received, payroll, the card's transactions and its latest statement. They're
  rendered from the events below in each provider's own layout (`scripts/benchmark/exports.ts`, written to
  `exports/`). Nothing is cleaned up. Payouts aren't matched to deposits, transfers aren't paired, and statements
  are newest-first. As in ChatGPT's and Claude's analysis tools, the files are uploaded, not pasted. The prompt lists
  them with their first lines, and the agent reads them with one tool, `run_python`, with pandas and numpy (see
  "The sandbox").
- **Arm B: Aggregate.** An agent connected to a local Aggregate that has imported the same events
  (`scripts/benchmark/serve.ts`: the server's clock is set to as-of and the demo is built with the settings below).
  It gets the server's read-only MCP tools (those marked `readOnlyHint`) and calls them itself.

Both arms see the same facts. Only how they reach them differs.

## The data

Harbor Coffee is a fictional café, generated by the demo connector (`@agfn/connector-demo`, profile `harbor`):

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| seed | `aggregate-bench-v1` |
| history start | `2024-10-01` (24 months) |
| Glass Ledger weeks from | `2024-10-01` |
| as of | `2026-09-30` (synced at `2026-09-30T22:00Z`) |

`docs/benchmark/events.ts` produces the stream: 6,192 events. It contains:

- Checking, savings, a cash drawer and a business card.
- Stripe: in-store sales and the Bean Club subscriptions, paid out daily.
- Shopify: online orders, paid out weekly.
- ADP payroll every two weeks.
- Rent, utilities and suppliers.
- Two supplier bills a week, paid from checking.

The traps a bookkeeper has to get right:

- **Payouts are not sales.** A Stripe deposit pays out a day of many charges, net of fees. Counting the deposit as sales double-counts and misses the fees.
- **Transfers between Harbor's own accounts aren't income.** This covers savings ↔ checking with no reference on either side, including a $15,000 top-up, and cash taken from the drawer to the bank.
- **Bills paid in two parts.** Bills paid a few cents short.
- **Mobile check deposits with no name.** These are catering orders paid by check.
- **Subscriptions.** Members join, upgrade and leave every month.

## Fiscal years

- "The fiscal year" is October 2025 through September 2026.
- "The prior fiscal year" is October 2024 through September 2025.

## Definitions used by the answers

- **Gross sales** are:
  - in-store card sales (Stripe charges) +
  - Bean Club charges (Stripe) +
  - online orders (Shopify) +
  - cash sales counted into the drawer,
  
  all before processor fees. They exclude:
  - payouts,
  - transfers,
  - cash deposits,
  - mobile check deposits (asked about separately).
- **Fees** are what Stripe or Shopify kept on each charge.
- **Balances** are the provider's own end-of-day balance on that date.
  - Card amounts are what is owed, as a positive number.
- **Bills owed** are bill totals less the bank payments made toward them by as-of. A bill paid a few cents short is still owed those cents.
- **MRR** is each active Bean Club subscription at its plan on that day. Upgrades count from the day they happened.
  - **Expansion** is the increase from upgrades in the month.
  - **Churned MRR** is the monthly value of members who left in the month.
  - **Net new** is new + expansion − churned. There are no downgrades or reactivations.

## Ground truth

`docs/benchmark/truth.ts` computes every answer directly from the events. For bills and planted cases, it also uses the generator that made them. It never uses Aggregate's ledger, metrics or tools, so the product doesn't grade itself.

The answers are written to `questions.json`. `scripts/benchmark-truth.test.ts` fails if a change to the generator would change any answer.

Each question has:

- `answer.type`: `money` (dollars), `percent` (e.g. `12.34`), `count`, `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD`) or `text`.
- `tolerance`:
  - `abs` in the answer's unit.
  - Exact sums use `0.01`.
  - Averages and percentages are looser, as listed.
  - `text` and `date` answers must match exactly; `text` is compared case-insensitively, trimmed.

## Scoring

- **Per question:** the model must end with one line, `ANSWER: <value>`. It's correct if within tolerance, else
  not correct. Scoring is automatic (`scripts/benchmark/score.ts`), with no model grading. Answers that aren't correct
  are reported in four groups:
  - **wrong:** a confident answer that doesn't match (the "confidently wrong" count).
  - **abstained:** `ANSWER: unknown`.
  - **no answer:** no `ANSWER:` line.
  - **error:** an API or harness failure after retries. Errors are re-run once, and any that remain are listed.
- **Score:** correct ÷ 100, overall and per category:
  - balances 12
  - sales 16
  - fees 8
  - payouts 10
  - transfers 10
  - expenses 16
  - bills 10
  - subscriptions 10
  - card 8
- **Also recorded per run:** model, arm, tokens, cost, wall time and tool calls.

## Freeze

1. Before any scored run, record one hash of these files in `FROZEN` (the runner refuses a scored run when they
   don't match it, and writes the hash into every run's `run.json`):
   - `questions.json`
   - this file
   - `config.json` (models and settings)
   - the exports in `exports/`
   - the harness in `scripts/benchmark/`
2. After that, nothing in those files changes for v1.
3. A fix becomes v2 with a new hash, and the results say which version they used.
4. **v2 (2026-10-12):** the v1 scorer had no case for `count` and `percent` answers (20 of the 100 questions), so
   those could never be marked correct. v2 adds both (exact for counts, the question's tolerance for percents) and a
   test that every question's own answer scores as correct. Arm B had already run under v1; its answers were
   re-judged from the saved transcripts with `rescore.ts` (no model was asked again), and the original scores are
   kept as `scores.original.jsonl` beside the new ones. Arm A runs under v2.
5. **v3 (2026-10-12):** a month answer now matches in any common format ("2026-08", "August 2026", "Aug 2026",
   "08/2026"). Under v2 the one month question (sales-11) was marked wrong in arm B for "2026-08" against
   "August 2026": the right month, written differently. Nothing else changes. Both arms are run again under v3 on
   the same build, so they're compared like for like; the v2 arm B run stays in the results as it was.

## The sandbox (arm A)

`run_python` is the same tool for every model, run by the harness (`scripts/benchmark/sandbox.ts`). The code is
written by a model, so it's treated as untrusted:

- **A throwaway container per question** (`scripts/benchmark/sandbox/Dockerfile`: Python 3.12 slim, pandas 2.2.3,
  numpy 2.1.3). Each call is a new Python process in it.
- **Sealed:** no network, a read-only root, the exports mounted read-only at `/data`, and 64 MB of writable `/tmp`.
  It runs as nobody, with all capabilities dropped.
- **Limits per call:** 512 MB memory, 1 CPU, 64 processes, 30 seconds, and output cut at 20,000 characters.
- **Recorded:** the image digest and the Python and pandas versions go into every run's `run.json`.

## Equal limits

Both arms get the same system prompt, question, sampling settings, at most 12 model turns, and at most 4,096 output tokens per
turn. The only difference is the tool: `run_python` over the files (A), or Aggregate's read-only tools (B).

## Models and settings

- **Model:** one Claude model, Claude Haiku 5.5 (`claude-haiku-5-5`), in both arms. `config.json` names it with the
  per-token prices used for the cost figures (from Anthropic's pricing page on the day the run was set up).
- **Prompt:** every model gets the same system prompt (`SYSTEM` in `run.ts`) and the same question text.
- **Temperature:** none is set. Claude 5.5 models don't accept one, so the model runs at its default sampling.
- **Caching:** Anthropic's prompt caching is on for the repeated prefix (system prompt, tool definitions). Caching
  changes the cost, not the content.

## Runs and budget

- **Runs:** each arm answers all 100 questions once.
- **Repeat:** a 10-question subset, fixed in `config.json` before the first run, is repeated to measure run-to-run
  variance.
- **Pilot:** a few questions first (`results/pilot-*`, not counted) check the harness and measure the cost.
- **Budget:** at most $25 in total (`BENCH_BUDGET_USD`, and the key's cap in Anthropic's console). Spending stops at
  the cap, and the summary says if it was reached.

## Publishing

Everything is published, including losses:

- these rules, the questions and answers, the harness and the exports;
- every transcript (`results/<run>/transcripts-*.jsonl`) and every score (`scores.jsonl`);
- the results table and chart (`results.md`, `chart.svg`).

If Aggregate's advantage is small, or absent in a category, the results say so.
